## Design Context

### Users
Groups of 2–6 housemates looking for a San Francisco "hacker house", often people relocating for South Bay jobs. They're busy, a little burned by scams and dead-end listings, and coordinating with each other. Each one fills in their constraints and connects their Google Calendar; together they read a short list of places verified by phone and accept a tour. The job: get to a few real, trustworthy options without weeks of scrolling and cold calls.

### Brand Personality
**Calm, precise, on-your-side.** Keyhunt is a concierge that already did the legwork: it speaks plainly, states facts with their evidence, and is honest when results are thin or housemates' wishes clash ("realistically, you said X"). The emotional goal is relief and trust, never urgency or hype. Copy is short and human, and never says "Verified" without proof.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Reference: Airbnb's warmth.** Friendly, consumer-grade, generous whitespace, rounded but not bubbly, real photos of places to give each listing a sense of home. Borrow the feel, not the brand: no Airbnb coral or logo-like shapes.
- **Evidence over gallery.** Shortlist items stay decision rows (price, commute, four checks, tour slot, "Hear the call") with a photo thumbnail for warmth, not a Zillow-style photo grid.
- **Theme: light only.** Warm off-white background, warm greys for text and lines.
- **Accent: one deep evergreen** for verified states, primary actions and the active pipeline stage. Status colors beyond it are muted (amber for "unverified / conflict", soft red only for scams).
- **Anti-references:** Zillow/listing-site grids, black-box "AI magic" with no reasoning shown, chatbot walls of paragraphs, mission-control dashboards, generic SaaS blue-purple gradients.

### Design Principles
1. **Show the proof.** Every claim links to the call moment that confirmed it; evidence is one click away, never buried.
2. **Calm by default.** One accent, restrained motion (pipeline counts and call reveals only), no fake spinners or urgency.
3. **The group, not the individual.** Make whose preference is whose visible, surface conflicts plainly, and only offer what works for everyone.
4. **Honest states.** Thin results, failed calls and ruled-out listings are shown with reasons and a next step, not hidden.
5. **Warm, not cute.** Friendly typography, photos and plain language, without playfulness that would undercut trust.

### Accessibility
Target WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast, visible focus, full keyboard use. Never rely on color alone for check status (pair with icon and label). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Call recordings always have a transcript.
